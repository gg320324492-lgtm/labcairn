# LC-P2-10 — Process annotation and the gold set — acceptance

**Work order:** LC-P2-10 (process annotation and the gold set) · **Subtasks:**
T00–T06 · **Task breakdown:**
[`../plans/LC-P2-10-task-breakdown.md`](../plans/LC-P2-10-task-breakdown.md) ·
**Status of the work order:** the code, the documents and **the annotations**
are landed.

**What this is.** The label set an annotator chooses from, the guide that
explains it, the synthetic corpus the annotations are read against, the
machinery that computes how far two independent readings agreed, and **the
annotations themselves** — ten items, two readings each, and an arbiter's
resolution.

**What this is not.** **It is not a human-annotated gold set.** The two
readings were made by **two independent Claude sessions**, and the arbiter
was a third; §5 reports the number they agreed on and §6 says what that
number does and does not measure. A human reading of the same corpus is the
comparison the number is meant to be read against, and it has not been made.

---

## 1. The decisions, and where each is held

**Four decisions shape this work order, and the fifth is not ours to make.**

| # | The decision | Held by | Witnessed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Three axes, and two addresses each.** A label says what problem the advisor pointed at, whether anything answered it, or whether the advice held — and it says it **separately for the comment element and for the body-side paragraph and offsets** | `src/labcairn/papers/labels.py` | `tests/test_gold_set_labels.py` |
| 2 | **The vocabulary is the chain's own, and it is imported rather than retyped.** The response states and the assessment outcomes come from the chain's modules; the issue kinds are the design's own twelve and no imported taxonomy's | `src/labcairn/papers/labels.py` | `test_the_labels_are_the_chains_own_vocabulary` |
| 3 | **`UNKNOWN` is an answer, and its spelling is part of the contract.** It is a member of every axis, it is scored as an answer, and the word is `unknown` | `src/labcairn/papers/labels.py` | `test_unknown_is_a_label_and_not_a_missing_value`, `test_unknown_is_spelled_unknown` |
| 4 | **The manifest is one contract with two verdicts.** Structure and completeness are different questions, and a resolution is a row of its own rather than an overwrite — **and the arbiter is the third reader, not one of the two** | `src/labcairn/papers/gold_manifest.py` | `tests/test_gold_set_manifest.py` |

**The fifth decision was not ours to make alone: what the labels actually
are.** An annotation is a reading of a manuscript, and LC-P2-10 builds the
vocabulary, the guide, the corpus and the machinery that computes the
agreement. **Filling in the readings was T06, and the owner decided on
2026-10-30 that two independent sessions would make them** — the same
reasoning that sent the corpus towards a pilot with invited annotators,
applied to a synthetic corpus where the alternative was an empty gold set.
**A gold set generated by the same code that generated the corpus measures
the generator**, and §6 says plainly what the machine reading measures and
what it does not.

## 2. The gap this work order found in its predecessor, and closed

**LC-P2-09 has no issue kinds, and this work order's own breakdown said it
did.** The breakdown's T00 reads *"The issue kinds are LC-P2-09's"* — and
the chain has no closed set of issue kinds to be. What it has is
`issue_key`: a **free-text key a person gives** to the issue a comment is
about, which the chain refuses to infer (`ChainRefusal.
ISSUE_KEY_MAY_NOT_BE_INFERRED`) precisely because the same wording is not
the same issue. **A key somebody types is not a vocabulary somebody can
choose from**, so the breakdown's premise was wrong, and it was wrong in a
way that would have been invisible: an implementation that took the issue
kinds from "somewhere" would have satisfied the sentence and produced a
gold set labelled in a language the chain does not speak.

**The real source is in the tree, and it is the design's own.** The upstream
study quotes it: *"The design's LC-P2-10 label set ('research question and
motivation, novelty and contribution, introduction and storyline, method
and hypothesis, data quality, controls and ablation, statistical conclusion,
figures, sources, review response, language and format, other/unknown') is a
**separate, wider** taxonomy, and the two are not interchangeable"* —
where "the two" are this set and arXivEdits' edit-intention classes. The
same passage is the reason the set is not imported: **arXivEdits' classes
describe what kind of text edit was made, which is a different axis from
what problem the advisor pointed at.**

**So the set is written down in code and tied to the record.** The witness
reads the design's set **out of that document** and asserts that every kind
in it is a label in the module — and a change to the set that nobody
recorded fails the test rather than passing it. **A vocabulary that lives in
one file and is checked against another is not somebody's memory of what the
design said.**

**Two places where the implementation is finer than the design's text, and
they are written down because they are decisions:**

- **The design writes `other/unknown` as one entry; the implementation has
  two labels.** `other` says the advisor pointed at something real that none
  of the twelve names — the reading was complete and the taxonomy was not
  enough. `unknown` says the annotator could not tell. **Counting them as
  one would score a gap in the taxonomy as a gap in the reading**, and the
  breakdown's own §2.1 puts `UNKNOWN` on every axis.
- **The design's text says nothing about two addresses.** The two-address
  rule is LC-P2-09's first clause, and the breakdown's §2.2 makes it the
  annotator's job: **a guide that asks for one label per comment loses the
  distinction the chain was built to keep.**

## 3. The clauses, and where each one is carried

**Ten clauses, and nine of them are carried by a test that fails when the
property is broken.** The one that is not is A9 — the guide's worked
examples are a reviewer's work, and a test that asserts a document exists
is not a read of a manuscript. **A10 used to be a reviewer's work too, and
stopped being one when the annotations landed**: the manifest's structure
now carries it, and the five witnesses its row names check that the two
readings are by two different annotators, that the arbiter is neither of
them, and that the arbiter's answer sits beside them rather than over them.
**What no witness can check is that the two readers never saw each other's
work** — that is a property of how the work was done, not of the file that
holds it, and §5 says how it was done.

**A8 has two witnesses, and the second one is the one that carries the
claim.** The first says the label set and the chain's vocabulary *agree*.
That is not the same as the label set *being* the chain's vocabulary: a
copy that is in step today agrees today, and a second spelling is exactly
what the module says it does not have. The second witness compares
**identity** — the label module's response states are the chain's object,
out of the chain's own module — and a hand-written constant with the same
four words is a different object, and fails.

**A10 has five witnesses, and they are not five views of one thing.** Two
of them watch the **rule** — the manifest refuses two readings by one
annotator, and refuses an arbiter who is one of them — and two watch the
**data** — the manifest in the tree has two different readers on every item
and an arbiter who is neither. **The fifth watches that the disagreement
survived.** Keeping the rule's witnesses and the data's witnesses apart is
the point: a rule that is never applied to the tree's own manifest is a
rule that has never been applied to anything, and **a witness that watches
the data can cover for a witness that watches the rule, and the other way
round.** M30–M32 were each run against both, and each turned only its own
red.

## 5. What was measured, and how

**Forty-six witnesses in four files, and thirty-two mutations against
them.**

| The file | The witnesses | What it watches |
|---|---|---|
| `tests/test_gold_set_labels.py` | 11 | the label set is closed, the vocabulary is the chain's own, `UNKNOWN` is an answer |
| `tests/test_gold_set_agreement.py` | 13 | the agreement is computed from the manifest, over a denominator it names |
| `tests/test_gold_set_manifest.py` | 13 | the manifest's own refusals, and that a resolution is a row rather than an overwrite |
| `tests/test_gold_set_corpus.py` | 9 | the corpus and the manifest agree, and the archives carry no clock |

**The corpus is four manuscripts in two projects, in two partitions, with
ten comments.** Every boundary case the writer can express is in it: a
comment whose span crosses a sentence and recurs under the same issue
key, a comment with no author, a comment with no date, a comment with no
range markers at all, an insertion, a deletion, and a comment whose
element and whose body-side anchor are about different problems.

**And the annotations: two independent readings that agreed on every
label.** T06's readings were made by **two separate Claude sessions**, each
alone with this guide and the corpus, and a third session wrote the
arbiter's row. **All sixty labels agree** — ten items × two addresses ×
three axes — and the six views `agreement()` reports are each 10/10.
**§6 records the one channel through which a hint reached two of the three
sessions, because an agreement number that hid it would be worth less than
one that says where it came from.**

**And a fourth, blind reading was run to measure what that hint did.** It
was given the manuscripts, the guide and the address package and nothing
else, and **it agreed with both readings on all twenty issue kinds** — and
**it found that the leak was still open in the guide**, which §6 says and
what it means for the four labels it cannot vouch for.

**That number is worth less than it looks, and the reasons are measured
rather than guessed.** The distribution says how much each view had to
choose between:

| The view | Distinct answers it held | What that means |
|---|---|---|
| `comment_address.issue_kind` | 7 | real choice |
| `body_address.issue_kind` | 7 | real choice |
| `comment_address.response_state` | 4 | real choice, on half the readings — the other half are `unknown` |
| `body_address.response_state` | 5 | the same |
| `comment_address.assessment_outcome` | **1** (`unknown`) | **no choice at all** |
| `body_address.assessment_outcome` | **1** (`unknown`) | **no choice at all** |

**The corpus holds no assessment record, no result figure and no review
response**, so the assessment axis has nothing to assess: every one of the
twenty readings is `unknown`, and its two views are 10/10 by default rather
than by judgement. **Two of the six views are therefore degenerate**, and a
report that said "100% agreement" without saying that would be selling a
number it had not earned.

**What the other four views mean is narrower than "the annotators
agreed".** The issue-kind views agree partly because the corpus's intent is
often the only defensible reading of a comment — the axis has little room.
**The response-state views are where the guide's boundaries could bite**,
and on the readings where there was something to decide the two sessions
still converged.

**And the two sessions converged for reasons of their own.** Each named the
same three items as its hardest, and each reached the same answer there
**by a different route** — one leaning on the guide's worked examples and on
the chain's own rule about what an assessment may rest on, the other on the
guide's distinction between a corpus with no later version and one with
nobody answering. **Two readers who arrived by different paths and stopped
in the same place is evidence of convergence; two readers who quoted the
same sentence would have been evidence of nothing.**

**The arbiter's own reading was made before it saw either of theirs, and
that is measured rather than reported.** The arbiter's output file was
written twice, and **the first write carried its ten independent rows with
its `items` rows still empty** — the file's own bytes are the evidence that
the comparison came second. Its independent rows are **identical to its
resolution rows on all sixty labels**, so the arbiter changed nothing: with
no disagreement to settle, its resolution *is* its own reading. **A
disagreement that did not happen is not a thing an arbiter arbitrated**, and
the record says so instead of implying a dispute the readings never had.

**And a rule the manifest did not have was found while checking A10.**
The manifest refused two readings by one annotator — one person reading
twice is one reading — and it said **nothing about the arbiter**. So a
manifest whose arbiter *was* one of its two annotators was accepted, and
**an arbiter who is one of the two has read the item twice rather than
arbitrated it**: the agreement number over that item would be a statement
about one reader, and the disagreement the arbiter was asked to settle
would have been settled by a party to it. **The tree's own manifest was
never in that state** — its arbiter is `agent-arbiter-1` and its readers
are `agent-annotator-a` and `agent-annotator-b` — but the rule's absence
is a hole in the contract that three readers share, and it is closed now:
`gold_manifest.py` refuses it by name, the tree's manifest is checked for
it directly, and three mutations (M30–M32) were run to show that the rule's
witness and the data's witness do not cover for each other.

**And the three sessions' transcripts were searched for what each one could
see.** The question was not "did the sessions say they were independent" —
they were asked not to read the answer key and both said they had not — but
"what did each session's transcript actually show it". **Zero reads of
`tests/gold_set_fixtures.py`, of `tests/docx_fixtures.py` or of the corpus
README's issue-kind table in any of the three sessions**, and **zero reads
of one annotator's output by the other**. The one channel that was open is
the manifest's `issue_key`, and §6 says what it did.

**The shard costs were re-measured from the runner's own log** — CI run
`41062950360`, on commit `5499ede`, its ten `python-quality` job logs read
for the per-file seconds pytest's `--durations=0` puts there. **The four
new test files are in the table with apportioned shares**, because the log
does not name them; the rest of the table is the runner's own measurement.
The partition that comes out of it is **the best five shards can do on
both platforms** — 1.000 on Windows, and 1.634 on Ubuntu, where the floor
is set by `tests/test_cli.py` alone (278 seconds of a 959-second suite).

**`ruff check`, `ruff format --check` and `mypy --strict` are clean**, and
**the full suite is green locally — 4165 passed and 5 skipped in 31
minutes** on the tree as it stands: the skips are a POSIX-only permission
check, a real-provider smoke that needs the owner's authorisation, one
Windows-drive check that is meaningless on POSIX, and two short-name checks
this volume does not have. **No CI run was started and no pull request was
opened** — the work is in the tree, and the owner's rule is that a run is
spent on a whole body of work rather than on each piece of it.

**Every number here is anchored to the tree it was measured on.** The shard
costs were read from the runner's log for `5499ede`; the local suite run and
the twenty-nine mutations were run on `claude/p2-lc-p2-10-plans` at
`1e601a8`, the commit before this record was amended. **The three later
mutations (M30–M32) were run separately, on the tree as it stands after the

that tree is not the one the other twenty-nine were measured on — so this
record names two trees rather than one.

## 6. The limits, and what is not done

**T06's readings exist, and they are not a person's work.** The manifest in
the tree carries two annotations per item and an arbiter's resolution on
every one, and `require_annotated` says so. **The annotators were three
Claude sessions, not three people** — two reading independently from the
guide, and a third that wrote its own reading first and compared it against
the two afterwards. §5 says what was measured about that, and §6 says what
the whole thing is worth.

**Three limits are worth knowing about, and none of them is a gap in the
work:**

- **The denominator's meaning is not distinguishable by its number.** The
  manifest requires two readings per item, so over any manifest the
  agreement function accepts, "the items both annotators labelled" and
  "the items in the manifest" are the same count. **The witness can
  therefore pin the number, pin the words that name what it counted, and
  pin the refusal to compute over a manifest with a single reading — but
  it cannot tell those two meanings apart.** That is a property of the
  domain, not of the witness: the two numbers are equal whenever the
  function is defined.
- **The Ubuntu partition is outside the balance limit, and that is
  structural.** `tests/test_cli.py` costs 278 seconds of a 959-second
  suite, so with five shards the best factor any arrangement reaches is
  `278 / (681 / 4) = 1.634` — above the 1.6 the limit asks for. **The
  partition is already at that floor**, so no rebalancing improves it.
  The cost check's slack came out negative, which made it report *every*
  changed file as one that could unbalance the partition — a quarter-second
  test file, reported as the reason a partition is lopsided. The check now
  asks the question that is true in that case, which is whether the changed
  file is the bottleneck, and two witnesses hold the new reading down.
  **The alternative — a partition that is inside the limit — would need
  either a different number of shards or a cheaper `test_cli.py`, and both
  are the owner's call rather than this work order's.**
- **Four of the shard costs were apportioned, not measured.** The runner
  has never run these four files, so their shares are what the tool's
  apportion rule gives them. They are fractions of a second against
  shards of three minutes, and the balance does not turn on them — but a
  runner that has run them would give better numbers, and the first CI run
  after this lands will.
- **A hint reached two of the three readers, and it named the answer.**
  The manifest carries an `issue_key` on the two comments that share a
  recurring issue, and the key's value was `iss-gold-a-significance` — a
  name that says the issue kind before a reader has read a word of the
  manuscript. **All three sessions read the manifest** (it is the contract
  the annotators fill in), and the transcripts were searched for the moment
  the key's wording entered each session: **it was in A's and B's context
  before either wrote its labels, and in the arbiter's only afterwards** —
  the arbiter read the manifest's structure without its values, wrote its
  own ten rows at 15:37:58, and opened A's and B's readings at 15:38:06.
  **B's own notes say it used the key**: *"[c401] — same issue kind as c302
  (the manifest also links both to `iss-gold-a-significance`)"*. So four of
  the sixty labels — the issue kinds on `c302` and `c401` — were made with
  the answer in reach, and **the record says so rather than reporting 60/60
  as if every label had been read blind.** The key was renamed to
  `iss-gold-a-r1` so the next reader gets no such hint, and the corpus
  README's issue-kind table — the author's own intentions, which are a
  second answer key — stayed out of all three sessions' reach, which the
  same transcript search confirms: **zero reads of the fixtures or of that
  README in any of the three sessions.**

**And the blind audit says what that hint did and did not do.** A fourth
session was given the manuscripts, the guide and the address package, and
**nothing else** — no manifest, no fixtures, no corpus README, no earlier
reading. It labelled the issue kind of all ten items on both addresses and
**agreed with A and B on every one of them: twenty out of twenty, three
ways.**

**But the audit also found the leak was still open, and it found it where
nobody had looked.** The guide's §1.0.1 — **the one section every annotator
is told to read first** — quoted the retired key by name, and said that two
comments in the corpus share one recurring issue. **So the renaming had
moved the leak rather than closed it**, and §1.0.1 narrowed the hint to a
pair: a reader with the old name in hand knew *which* two comments it was
about. **The audit's judgement is that the pair's agreement is therefore
not evidence of independence** — a reader holding that name could have
written `statistical_conclusion` without reading either manuscript — while
**the other eight items' agreement is clean**, because the name was about
the pair and nothing else. The §1.0.1 text no longer quotes the retired
name, and the retired name is now written down **only in this record**,
where it is evidence rather than a hint.

**So the honest position is: sixteen of the twenty issue-kind labels have
a measured independent agreement, and four do not.** The four are the issue
kinds on `c302` and `c401` — the pair the retired name pointed at. **The
blind audit's agreement on those four is not counted as evidence**, because
the reader that produced it had the name in reach. **Closing that last gap
needs a re-read of those two items by a session that has never seen the
retired name**, and the guide's §3.7 worked example is the same shape as
both of them, so a reader of the guide alone could reach the same label
from the example rather than from the manuscripts. **That is the next
measurement, and this record does not claim it.**

**Two smaller things, recorded because they are choices and not
oversights:** the writer cannot express a comment's range more narrowly
than a paragraph, so the manifest's body-side addresses are
paragraph-level and the topic phrase is the corpus's own record; and the
corpus carries no comment with only a start marker or only an end
marker, because the guide has no rule for one and **a corpus case the
guide cannot answer is a broken gold set**.
